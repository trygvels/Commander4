## 3. Verdicts

### Fact (ii): sandwich variance with a noise-noise term beyond the inverse information

- States it explicitly, functional maximum likelihood with known error covariance (the paper's estimator): Engel (1985) Eq. (21), citing Patefield (1977, 1978), read for the
  earlier note: (1/T) (sigma^2/m_0^2) {psi m_0 + (1 - rho^2) phi^2 sigma^2}, "not the same as those derived from Fisher's information matrix".
- States it explicitly, moment-corrected estimator with known unequal error variances: ref. 4, Sect. 2 (pp. 8-9): psi_22 = [2 beta_1^2 (tau_x** - nu_2^2) + pi] / nu_2^2, pi =
  beta_1^2 nu_2 tau_x* + sigma^2 nu_2 + tau_xy* + sigma^2 tau_x* + nu_2 tau_y* + 2 beta_1^2 nu_2^2, with tau_xy* the mean product of the two error variances and tau_x** the mean
  square of the x error variance; "these expressions do not depend on nu_3 and nu_4". Eq. (2.7) is the sandwich A_n^-1 B_n A_n^-1 ("see Cheng and Riu (2006)"), sum(-X_i^2 + tau_xi) in A_n.
- States the form: ref. 7, Sect. 5.1, corrected-score estimator: "Its asymptotic covariance matrix is given by the sandwich formula" (1/n) A^-1 B A^-T, A = -E(d psi_CS / d beta), B =
  E(psi_CS psi_CS^T). Ref. 6, Theorem 1, Eqs. (3.4), (3.7), (3.8): avar = I^-1 + I^-1 (sigma^-4 B) I^-1, B proportional to the error covariance, "an extension of Nakamura ((1990),
  equations (3) and (4))". Ref. 3, Theorems 1, 2 and Remark 2: variance = sample variance of u_i over (S_xx - theta)^2, tending to the variances of Cheng and Tsai (1995) and Fuller (1987).
- Implies it: ref. 13, Eqs. (11)-(15) (empirical variance of terms with the debiased variance in the denominator and beta_1 V_11,i - V_12,i in the numerator). Ref. 10, abstract:
  consistency, asymptotic normality and confidence regions for the functional estimator, no formula.
- Could not confirm: the formula in Fuller (1987) or Gleser (1981); the wording "overstates the information by 1 + T/J" anywhere. Ref. 14, Sect. 7.1 (HTML-S) says the like for
  ordinary least squares only: its variance "decreases as the measurement errors [...] increase, giving one the false impression" of precision.
- (inference) For x = a + u, y = beta a + e, uncorrelated errors, s_vv = s_ee + beta^2 s_uu, J = n m_0 / s_vv: Engel's Eq. (21) is J^-1 (J + T) J^-1 with T = n s_uu s_ee / s_vv^2;
  ref. 4 (equal variances, no equation error) has T = n (s_uu s_ee + 2 beta^2 s_uu^2) / s_vv^2. Same structure, different T; the profile estimator matches the first.

### Fact (iii): moment or corrected-score debiasing (subtract the known noise term)

- States it explicitly: ref. 13, Eq. (6) V(Y_ki) = V(X_ki) + E(V_kk,i), Eq. (9), and p. 8: "the observed variance of Y_1 is biased necessitating the 'debiased' sample variance seen
  in the denominator". Ref. 7, Sect. 4: beta_C = s_xy / (s_x^2 - sigma_delta^2) "is a consistent estimator of beta". Ref. 4, Eq. (2.2). Ref. 6, Eqs. (2.12), (2.13): corrected observed
  information I* = sigma^-2 {X^T V^-1 X - tr(V^-1) Lambda}, E*{I*} = sigma^-2 Z^T V^-1 Z (Z the true regressors); for V = I this is "the corrected log likelihood for normal regression
  models proposed by Nakamura ((1990), p. 131)". Ref. 7, Sect. 5.1: E[psi_CS(y, x; b) | y, xi] = psi(y, xi; b) "(Nakamura, 1990)".
- Implies it or is cited for it: Fuller (1987) Sect. 1.2.1 (title; refs. 3 and 14 attribute the moment correction to it); Nakamura (1990) abstract (HTML-S); Carroll et al. (2006)
  section titles "Correcting for Bias" and "Corrected Score Functions" (chapter numbers not confirmed); Cheng and Van Ness (1999, Chap. 3) as cited by ref. 4.
- (inference) Fact (iii) debiases the information used for the error, not the estimator. Its closest classical object is Nakamura's corrected observed information; in the classical
  moment estimator the same subtraction defines the estimator.
