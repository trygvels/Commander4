## 2. Synchrotron beta_s sampling (polarization, BP XV; intensity, BP XIII)

### BP XV, 2011.08503 (polarization)

- Regions. Abstract: "We partition the sky into four large disjoint regions (High Latitude; Galactic Spur; Galactic Plane; and Galactic Center)". Sect. 2.1.1 (quoted): "We started from the 24-region partitioning defined by Fuskeland et al. (2014)" and "This process resulted in four main regions, which we will refer to as 1) High Latitudes;" (then Spur, Galactic Center, Galactic plane). Fig. 1 shows the outlines.
- Data. Sect. 3 (quoted): "we include only the WMAP Ka-, Q-, and V-band channels in the following, centered on 33, 41 and 61 GHz"; "we choose to include only the Planck HFI 857 GHz channel in temperature and the 353 GHz channel in polarization"; "In addition, we include the Haslam 408 MHz survey to constrain synchrotron emission in temperature"; "This results in a total of eight frequency channels in intensity, and seven frequency channels in polarization" (LFI 30, 44, 70, WMAP Ka, Q, V, HFI 353 in polarization). "we consider in the following only the low-resolution WMAP polarization data at a HEALPix" Nside 16 "for which dense pixel-pixel noise covariance matrices are available"; "All data except the Planck DR4 353 GHz channel are analyzed as provided by the respective team" "without any preprocessing or smoothing."; 353 GHz "is smoothed to an angular resolution of 10'" and "re-pixelized at a resolution of Nside=1024".
- Stokes parameters. Sect. 5.1 (quoted): the chi-square map is "summed over Stokes Q and U." Eq. 26 is in vector form over all frequency maps; it does not single out Q and U explicitly.
- Likelihood. Sect. 4.3, Eq. 26 (as extracted): P(beta | d, a) propto [ prod_nu exp( -1/2 (d_nu - A_nu(beta) a)^t N_nu^-1 (d_nu - A_nu(beta) a) ) ] P(beta), with "the set of all available frequency maps". Sect. 4.3 (quoted): "Since WMAP is pixelized with Nside=16" "the chi^2 map is too." Sect. 4.3 also says the smoothed region map is used to "evaluate the mixing matrix" "at the appropriate resolution for each frequency channel." The abstract (quoted): "This analysis represents the first implementation of true multi-resolution component separation".
- Smoothing. Sect. 4.3 (quoted): proposals are made "independently for each region i", then "smooth the resulting map with a 10 degree FWHM Gaussian beam to suppress edge effects."; "This smoothed map is then used to evaluate the mixing matrix at the appropriate resolution for each frequency channel." (The 4 degree FWHM in Sect. 2.1.1 applies to the processing mask, not the region map.)
- Priors. Sect. 4.4 (quoted): "we adopt for the main analysis Gaussian priors of beta_s = -3.3 +- 0.1 and beta_d = 1.56 +- 0.10, respectively."; "While the Spur and Galactic plane regions are fitted using both the likelihood and prior as described above, we only include the prior for the high-Galactic and Galactic Center regions."; "a broader prior width of sigma_beta = 0.2, which permits synchrotron indices as shallow as beta_s = -2.7" (described as producing foreground excess). Same prior mean and width for all regions: the text gives one prior, no per-region values.
- Sampled vs prior-drawn. Fig. 9 caption (quoted): "Dotted lines indicate regions that are sampled exclusively from the prior distribution, while solid lines indicate regions that are sampled from the full posterior distribution." Sect. 5.3.1 (quoted): the Galactic Center "immediately converges to a very low mean value of beta_s = -4.15 +- 0.05" in the nine-region test (prior -2.8 +- 0.1), so "we instead assign the spectral index of this region a physically meaningful value, as defined by the prior"; Sect. 5.3.2 on High Latitude: "this region is also both the most important region for CMB purposes, and it is prior-dominated." and "we therefore sample this from the prior alone, as for the Galactic Center."
- Algorithm and cadence. Sect. 4.3 (quoted): "we adopt a standard Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler for beta."; proposal "T(beta^(i)|beta^(i-1)) ~ N(beta^(i-1), C_beta), where C_beta is a tunable proposal matrix."; "Before starting a full Gibbs analysis, we perform a tuning run using a diagonal" C_beta and "adjust the diagonal elements until the resulting accept rate is between 0.2 and 0.8."; "Once that happens, we run a longer chain with fixed" C_beta and "replace this matrix with the covariance matrix from the resulting samples."; the tunable "the number of Metropolis steps per main Gibbs iteration, n" is set so that the correlation between beta^(i) and beta^(i+n) "is less than 0.1." So beta_s is updated every main Gibbs iteration with n Metropolis steps; the numerical value of n is not stated.
- Results. Sect. 5.3.2 (quoted): "For these, we find posterior mean and standard deviations of beta_s^Spur = -3.17 +- 0.06 and beta_s^Plane = -3.03 +- 0.07."; both "have shallower mean spectral indices than the prior of" -3.3; "In short, the Spur and Galactic plane regions are signal-dominated, and the prior is of limited importance."; "shifting the prior mean by as much as Delta_p beta = 0.5 only affects the posterior by Delta beta <~ 0.1."; for a 0.2 shift "the final posterior shifts will be Delta beta <~ 0.03"; "The steepening is however only statistically significant at the 2 sigma level". Planck DR4 map check: results "individually statistically consistent with the BeyondPlanck results at the 0.5 sigma level." High Latitude and Galactic Center: posterior equals the prior by construction (prior-only sampling), no separate numbers given. Table of per-region values: none in the paper; values appear in text and in Figs. 9, 12, 13.
- Posterior vs prior width for beta_s: posterior sd 0.06 to 0.07 vs prior sd 0.1 for Spur and Plane (numbers from Sect. 5.3.2 and 4.4; the comparison itself is mine (inference)). The paper makes the explicit width comparison only for dust, Sect. 5.3.3 (quoted): "the uncertainty is significantly smaller than the prior width, which suggests that the result is indeed data-driven."
- Dust. Sect. 2.1.2: a single spatially constant beta_d, T_d fixed per pixel from Planck. Sect. 5.3.3 (quoted): "we adopt a prior of beta_d = 1.56 +- 0.10"; posterior "may be reasonably approximated as a Gaussian with beta_d = 1.62 +- 0.04." The abstract states beta_d = 1.64 +- 0.03. Both numbers are in the paper; the body text (Sect. 5.3.3) and abstract disagree at the last digit.
- Simulation recovery test of beta_s: not present in BP XV (consistent with research/2026-10-27_index_field_recovery_prior_art.md).

### BP XIII, 2201.08188 (intensity)

- Table 2: beta_s ~ N(-3.3 +- 0.1), single full-sky value; curvature C = 0. Sect. 6.1 (quoted): prior "as derived from polarization measurements"; chi^2 rises sharply for beta_s <~ -2.7 and is nearly flat below about -3.2; because the chi^2 variations exceed the prior and appear to absorb dust modelling errors, "we instead chose to marginalize explicitly over the prior and disable the likelihood term entirely." and they "did not attempt to constrain beta_s with the current data set." ## 3. Amplitudes during the index step; mixing between a and beta

- BP XV, Sect. 4.3 (quoted): "In general, a and beta are strongly correlated for a given component" "especially for high signal-to-noise data."; joint (marginal) sampling "is only computationally practical for observations with the same angular resolution", "all data maps must be smoothed to a common angular resolution", "the smoothing operation correlates the instrumental noise", and since the data include "low-resolution WMAP data with dense noise covariance matrices" "smoothing all data to this resolution is impractical." The conditional P(beta | d, a) of Eq. 26 is derived "recalling that the amplitude is for the moment assumed to be perfectly known." So in polarization, amplitudes are held fixed during the beta_s step (conditional Gibbs).
- BP I, Sect. 8.2 (quoted): "we first sample beta marginalized over a, and then a conditionally on beta"; "For polarization, we still sample beta conditionally on a, as described by Svalheim et al. 2022b"; "Since a is a set of linear parameters, the integral over a may be computed analytically". Sect. 8.3.5 (quoted): "the linear amplitudes a and spectral parameters beta are in general highly degenerate for high signal-to-noise models."; "We therefore use this expression only for intensity sampling, coupled to a tuned Metropolis sampler." Polarization sampler "is conditional on the foreground amplitudes."
- BP III, Sect. 2.1, Eqs. 3 to 9 (read from PDF): the Gibbs chain lists beta <- P(beta | d, g, n_corr, xi_n, Delta_bp, C_l) (Eq. 7, a absent from the conditioning set) followed by a <- P(a | d, g, n_corr, xi_n, Delta_bp, beta, C_l) (Eq. 8). The text does not comment on the omission of a in Eq. 7. (inference: Eq. 7 as written matches the intensity marginal step of BP XIII.)
- BP XIII, Sect. 4.1 and Fig. 1 (quoted): toy "two-dimensional Gaussian distribution" with "Pearson's correlation coefficient of rho = 0.99"; "Both cases show the first 100 samples initialized from the purple point."; for pure Gibbs "We see that less than half of the distribution is actually explored, and many thousands of samples will be required"; for the joint sampler the 100 samples "cover the full distribution very efficiently." Procedure (quoted): "First, we run a short (typically a few hundred steps) standard Metropolis sampler"; "Immediately following the last Metropolis step, we draw one sample from" P(a | m, beta) (Eq. 24); "it is critically important that no other parameters are updated between" "beta and a, as the previous value of a is completely inconsistent with the new beta value, which is drawn marginally with respect to a."
- Numerical Gibbs correlation lengths between amplitudes and indices: not stated in any of the four papers. The only quantitative statements are BP XV Sect. 5.3.2 (beta_s correlation length 30 to 50 Gibbs samples in the full chain) and BP XIII Sect. 6.2 (Fig. 17 correlation matrix: "very high correlation between beta_d and both the AME peak frequency nu_p and the CMB dipole amplitude"; "all microwave frequency monopoles are internally strongly correlated" and "anticorrelated with the component monopoles").

## 4. BP XIII amplitude-marginalized likelihood (Sect. 4.1, Eqs. 19 to 25)

- Eq. 19: P(a, beta | m_nu) = P(beta | m_nu) P(a | m_nu, beta); "we may generate a joint sample by first drawing beta from its 'marginal' distribution" then a from the conditional.
- Eq. 22 (raw characters): "ln L(a,beta) propto -1/2 sum_nu (m_nu - A_nu(beta) a)^T N_nu^-1 (m_nu - A_nu(beta) a)". Assumption (quoted): "we assume no spatial amplitude priors" (S^-1 = 0).
- Eq. 23 (raw characters as rendered, three lines):
  "ln L_marg(beta) = ln int d a exp[ -1/2 (m - A a)^T N^-1 (m - A a) ]"
  "= const - 1/2 (A^T N^-1 m)^T (A^T N^-1 A)^-1 (A^T N^-1 m)"
  "+ 1/2 ln | (A^T N^-1 A)^-1 |,"
  followed by (quoted) "where all terms should be interpreted as sums over frequencies." Marginalized over: the amplitude vector a of all components, with a flat (no) prior. Determinant term: plus one half of the log of the determinant of (A^T N^-1 A)^-1, written with absolute-value bars, not "det". Resolution (quoted): "We note that this expression requires all data to be defined at the same angular resolution"; "All data are smoothed to a common angular and pixel resolution before evaluating the expression."
- Sign check (inference): completing the square in the Gaussian integral gives + 1/2 (A^T N^-1 m)^T (A^T N^-1 A)^-1 (A^T N^-1 m) - 1/2 ln|A^T N^-1 A|, i.e. the quadratic term should carry a plus sign and the determinant term as written (+ 1/2 ln|(A^T N^-1 A)^-1|) is consistent with that. The minus sign on the quadratic term in the rendered Eq. 23 is either a typo in the paper or an extraction artifact; verify against the PDF before relying on it.
- Ridge likelihood (quoted): "The same authors also introduced a so-called 'ridge likelihood,' in which one does not marginalize over a," "but rather sets a equal to its maximum likelihood value for a given value of beta." "This may also be analytically evaluated, and is in fact identical to the above expression," "with the exception of the last determinant term being excluded." Source attribution (quoted): "this was done by Stompor et al. 2009 and Stivoli et al. 2010 as part of developing the Miramare component separation code."
- Eq. 24 (amplitude draw after the Metropolis steps): (sum_nu A_nu^t N_nu^-1 A_nu) a = sum_nu A_nu^t N_nu^-1 m_nu + sum_nu A_nu^t N_nu^-1/2 eta. Eq. 25 adds a Gaussian amplitude prior (S^-1, mu, eta_2).
- Where it was used: Sect. 4.1: drives the Metropolis step for each spectral parameter in the intensity joint sampler; Sect. 4.3 and Fig. 5: demonstrated on nu_p in an AME-only simulation (10 degree FWHM, Nside 16); Sect. 6.1: in the production run the likelihood is disabled for beta_s, beta_d (prior only) and T_e (fixed at 7000 K), so nu_p is "the only spectral parameter that is fully sampled from the data" (Sect. 6.4). BP I Sect. 8.3.5, Eq. 118 presents the same marginal form for intensity (rendered as "-2 ln P(beta|m) = sum_nu (A_nu^t N_nu^-1 m_nu)^t (A_nu^t N_nu^-1 A_nu)^-1 (A_nu^t N_nu^-1 m_nu)" with no determinant term and introduced by "Given this expression, the marginal log-posterior for beta then reads (Stompor et al. 2009)"). (inference: as rendered, BP I Eq. 118 lacks the log-determinant and has the opposite sign on the quadratic term relative to a correct marginalization; treat the rendering as unverified.) BP I Eq. 119 (polarization): "-2 ln P(beta | m, a) = sum_nu ((m_nu - A_nu(beta) a) / sigma_nu(p))^2".
- Not used for polarization: BP XV uses the conditional Eq. 26 only (Sect. 4.3).

## 5. Monopoles and zero levels

- BP XIII, Sect. 4.2 "Component-based monopole determination" (quoted): "it is, in fact, much simpler to determine the monopoles of the component amplitude maps."; "The frequency map zero-levels have thus no independent impact on any higher order analyses."; "This must be done on a case-by-case basis, applying the most natural prior for each component."; CMB: "The monopole of the CMB component map is set to zero (or 2.7255 K) outside this mask." (mask f_sky = 0.64); free-free: zero mean outside a mask with f_sky = 0.50; synchrotron: 8.9 +- 1.3 K_CMB at 408 MHz from Wehus et al. (2017), "We marginalized over the uncertainty by drawing a random offset correction in every Gibbs iteration."; dust and AME offsets from HI column-density cross-correlation (dust thresholds 1.5 to 4 x 10^20 cm^-2, AME 2 to 4 x 10^20 cm^-2 at Nside 16 and 10 degree FWHM, intersection drawn from a Gaussian); frequency-band monopoles: "fit and subtract the residual monopole outside some mask" after subtracting the sky model.
- BP XIII, Sect. 4.3 "Joint spectral parameter and frequency-band monopole sampling" (quoted): "We therefore implement internal estimation of frequency band monopoles during the spectral parameter sampling algorithm," "such that for each proposed spectral parameter value, we estimate a new band monopole value" "conditioned on the input amplitude and the proposed parameter value."; "The frequency band monopoles are updated together with the final parameter at the end of the sampling."; "We see that by marginalizing over the band monopoles the log-likelihood function widens by a factor of 1.5-2." (Fig. 5). So band monopoles couple directly to the spectral parameter (nu_p) step.
- BP XIII, Sect. 6.2 (quoted): "all microwave frequency monopoles are internally strongly correlated" and anticorrelated with the component monopoles (changing one component monopole requires the band monopoles to offset it).
- BP XIII, Sect. 6.4 and Table 3 (uK_CMB; 857 GHz in K_CMB), BeyondPlanck vs Planck 2015: LFI 30: 0 +- 6 vs -17 +- 1; LFI 44: 0 +- 2 vs 11 +- 1; LFI 70: 0 +- 1 vs 16 +- 1; WMAP Ka: 16 +- 3 vs 33 (fixed in Planck 2015); Q1: 12 +- 2 vs 2 +- 1; Q2: 11 +- 2 vs 2 +- 1; V1: 6 +- 1 vs 1 +- 1; V2: 6 +- 1 vs 1 +- 1; HFI 857: -0.65 +- 0.03 vs -0.72 +- 0.01 (Planck DR4). (quoted): "the BeyondPlanck LFI mean monopoles are all zero; this happens by construction during the mapmaking process."; "the predicted monopole at WMAP Ka-band is 11 uK_CMB with beta_s = -3.1 and 4 uK_CMB with beta_s = -3.3," a 7 uK_CMB difference the band monopoles must absorb. This is the explicit statement of monopole and beta_s coupling.
- BP I, Sect. 8.1 (quoted): "impose prior knowledge regarding the CIB zero-level amplitude at each frequency during component separation". Sect. 5.3.3: monopole and dipole corrections only for Haslam 408 MHz (Wehus et al. 2017). No further monopole treatment found in Sects. 1 to 9.1.
- BP III, Listing 2 (prototype parameter file): "COMP_MONOPOLE_PRIOR = monopole-dipole:mask.fits" for the CMB component. No description of monopole sampling in the text.
- BP XV: no statement about frequency-map monopoles or zero levels found in the full text; the only "zero" is the amplitude prior mean "we set a-bar = 0" (Sect. 4.2.2).

## 6. AME peak frequency nu_p

- BP XIII, Table 2: nu_p ~ N(22 +- 3 GHz), single full-sky value. Sect. 6.1 (quoted): "the chi^2 is well-defined with a typical best-fit value around 22 GHz."; "It is clear that the current data combination has significant constraining power for nu_p."; sampling mask "The accepted sky fraction of the resulting mask is f_AME = 0.91." (earlier masks f_AME = 0.66). Sect. 6.4 (quoted): "we find a posterior mean and standard deviation of nu_p = 25.3 +- 0.5 GHz."; "the only spectral parameter that is fully sampled from the data"; result depends heavily on thermal dust assumptions. Likelihood: the marginal Eq. 23 with joint band-monopole estimation (Sects. 4.1, 4.3), few hundred Metropolis steps then one amplitude draw. Posterior width 0.5 GHz vs prior width 3 GHz (numbers from Table 2 and Sect. 6.4; comparison is mine (inference)). Sect. 5 (quoted): in the map-domain simulation "the only spectral parameter which is sampled and constrained by data is the AME peak frequency." with "excellent agreement with the input values for both spectral parameters and the band monopoles."
- BP XIII, Sect. 6.2: beta_d is "very highly correlated" with nu_p and the CMB dipole amplitude; preliminary fits with free beta_d drove beta_d above 2 and pushed nu_p to unphysically high frequencies (Sect. 6.1), which is why beta_d is prior-only.
- BP I, Sect. 3.3.4 (quoted): "we introduce a peak position parameter, nu_p, that shifts the spectrum rigidly."; Sect. 8.3.5: all spectral parameters other than beta_s are "a single constant value across the full sky." nu_p result in BP I Sect. 9.2+: not read.
- BP XV: nu_p not sampled (polarization only; no AME component in the polarization model).
- BP III: not stated beyond the module list (comm_spindust_comp_mod "SpdustSED with nu_p", Fig. 2).
