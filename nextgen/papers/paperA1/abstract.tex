\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In pixel-based Bayesian component separation a few spectral indices share the data with a very large
number of per-pixel amplitudes. We study what three common ways of removing the amplitudes do to
the index. (1) A Metropolis move that proposes an index, redraws the amplitudes and accepts on the
$\chi^2$ of the new pair is, on its own, a pseudo-marginal kernel for the profile posterior; inside a
Gibbs sweep that also redraws the amplitudes it becomes Monte Carlo within Metropolis and converges to
the profile posterior raised to the power $1/T$, with $T = \tfrac12 + 1/(4c_k) \simeq \sqrt{\pi k/8} +
\tfrac12$ for $k$ amplitudes. The index error from such a chain is too wide by $0.79\,k^{1/4}$. We
derive the law, its form for several moves per amplitude draw and for inexact stored amplitudes,
confirm it on toy chains to 0.2 percent, and use it to account for a real-map chain. (2) A flat amplitude prior is exactly
an exponential prior on the index with rate $n\ln\nu_{\rm ref}$, $n$ the number of amplitudes and
$\nu_{\rm ref}$ the frequency at which they are defined. The index is shifted by
$\ln(\nu_{\rm ref}/\nu_{\rm piv})$ times the index variance carried by one amplitude, a shift that does
not average down over pixels, changes sign across a pivot frequency set by the bands and by the prior
on the CMB, and has opposite signs for references of 23 and 30 GHz with the WMAP and Planck LFI bands.
(3) The profile likelihood has an exactly unbiased score, but its curvature is too sharp by
$\sqrt{1+\Tm/\J}$, where $\Tm/\J$ is the inverse mean squared signal-to-noise ratio of the amplitudes; the
natural plug-in sandwich correction is biased the wrong way, and a debiased form gives nominal coverage
over four decades of $\Tm/\J$. A zero-mean amplitude prior with a free scale is the same estimator for
a uniform noise pattern and is biased by up to 0.6 standard deviations otherwise. We apply the
estimators side by side to the WMAP nine-year K to V polarization maps with their dense noise
covariances and to the Planck 30 to 70 GHz maps. On the same maps, regions and noise model, the
high-latitude polarized synchrotron index is $-3.09$ with the profile likelihood, $-3.10$ with a
flat amplitude prior stated at 23 GHz and $-2.94$ with the same prior stated at 30 GHz: twelve
standard deviations from a change of convention, as the identity predicts to 8 percent. For a band
set without K band and with an unconstrained CMB amplitude, the flat-prior optimum at a 30 GHz
reference lies at the upper edge of the allowed range at high latitude, more than a unit of index
from the profile optimum, which offers a reading of the flat high-latitude indices reported by
earlier analyses in that configuration. Two instrumental effects turn out to matter at the level of
the statistical errors: the pixel scale of the low-resolution maps carries a beam-aliasing bias
toward flat indices, of 0.02 to 0.07, and the Planck 30 GHz polarization map cannot be combined with
WMAP in one power law, its index against K band differing by 0.5 between the 2018 and PR4 releases.
From WMAP K to V at multipoles up to 20 we find $\beta_{\rm s} = -3.11 \pm 0.01$ at high latitude,
$-3.11 \pm 0.02$ in the North Polar Spur and $-3.06 \pm 0.01$ in the Galactic plane (statistical).
\end{abstract}
