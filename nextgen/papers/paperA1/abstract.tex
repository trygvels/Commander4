\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In pixel-based Bayesian component separation a few spectral indices share the data with a very large
number of per-pixel amplitudes. We study what three common ways of removing the amplitudes do to
the index. (1) A Metropolis move that proposes an index, redraws the amplitudes and accepts on the
$\chi^2$ of the new pair is, on its own, a pseudo-marginal kernel for the profile posterior; inside a
Gibbs sweep that also redraws the amplitudes it becomes Monte Carlo within Metropolis and, for small
steps, converges to the profile posterior raised to the power $1/T$, with $T = \tfrac12 + 1/(4c_k)
\simeq \sqrt{\pi k/8} + \tfrac12$ for $k$ amplitudes. The index error from such a chain is too wide by
$0.79\,k^{1/4}$. We derive the law and its form for several moves per amplitude draw and for inexact
stored amplitudes, and confirm it against an exact-kernel calculation and simulated chains within
their Monte Carlo errors of 0.4 percent. (2) Moving the frequency at which a flat amplitude prior is
stated multiplies the index prior by an exponential with rate $n\ln(\nu_{\rm ref}'/\nu_{\rm ref})$,
$n$ the number of amplitudes. For one component the index is shifted by
$\ln(\nu_{\rm ref}/\nu_{\rm piv})$ times the index variance carried by one amplitude, a shift that
does not average down over pixels, changes sign across a pivot frequency set by the bands and by the
prior on the CMB, and, when the CMB amplitude is constrained, has opposite signs for references of 23
and 30 GHz with the WMAP and Planck LFI bands. (3) The profile likelihood has an exactly unbiased score, but its curvature is too sharp
by $\sqrt{1+\Tm/\J}$, where $\Tm/\J$ is the inverse mean squared signal-to-noise ratio of the amplitudes;
the natural plug-in sandwich correction is biased the wrong way, and a debiased form gives nominal
coverage over four decades of $\Tm/\J$. For one component and a uniform noise pattern a zero-mean
amplitude prior with a free scale is the same estimator; otherwise it is biased, by up to 0.6 standard
deviations in controlled tests and by one standard deviation in simulations of the configuration below.
We then apply the estimators side by side to the WMAP nine-year K to V polarization maps with their
dense noise covariances, a $\Lambda$CDM prior on the CMB, four regions defined by geometry, and the
brightest emission and Tau A masked. On the same maps, regions and noise model the polarized
synchrotron index at $|b| > 30^\circ$ is \N{prof2.HL} with the profile likelihood, \N{f23two.HL} with
a flat amplitude prior stated at 23 GHz and \N{f30two.HL} with the same prior stated at 30 GHz. The
change of convention moves the index by \N{d3023two.range} in the four regions, \N{d3023sd.range}
statistical standard deviations, and the first-order form of the identity predicts every move, too
high by \N{predH.over.range} percent. With a free CMB amplitude the flat-prior marginal has no
optimum at high latitude in either convention: the index runs to the edge of the allowed range,
$-1.6$, where the profile likelihood gives \N{flat.prof2.HL}. The zero-mean prior lands \N{zmsd.abs.HL} standard deviations from the profile
optimum at high latitude. A white prior on the CMB in place of the $\Lambda$CDM covariance, with nearly
the same total variance, makes the error bar too small by \N{mm.percent.range} percent, by an exact
first-order calculation that we give. With the mask no region is misfitted and errors
from the scatter of the score over patches of sky agree with the model's. We do not quote an index:
it moves by \pend{sizes} with the angular scales that inform it, the prior on the CMB and the fixed
dust index; and an earlier version of this analysis, on regions reconstructed from a published index
map and without a mask, had regions that did not fit and one region whose index a single source,
Tau A, moved by \N{tauA.absmove.Plane}, six of its statistical errors.
\end{abstract}
