\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In pixel-based Bayesian component separation a few spectral indices share the data with a very large
number of per-pixel amplitudes. We study what three common ways of removing the amplitudes do to
the index. (1) A Metropolis move that proposes an index, redraws the amplitudes and accepts on the
$\chi^2$ of the new pair, used inside a Gibbs sweep, converges for small steps to the profile
posterior raised to the power $1/T$, with $T = \tfrac12 + 1/(4c_k) \simeq \sqrt{\pi k/8} + \tfrac12$
for $k$ amplitudes: the index error from such a chain is too wide by $0.79\,k^{1/4}$. (2) Moving the
frequency at which a flat amplitude prior is stated multiplies the index prior by an exponential
with rate $n\ln(\nu_{\rm ref}'/\nu_{\rm ref})$ for $n$ amplitudes. The index is shifted by
$\ln(\nu_{\rm ref}/\nu_{\rm piv})$ times the index variance carried by one amplitude, a shift that
does not average down over pixels and changes sign across a pivot frequency set by the bands and by
the prior on the CMB. (3) The profile likelihood has an unbiased score and a curvature that is too
sharp by $\sqrt{1+\Tm/\J}$, with $\Tm/\J$ the inverse mean squared signal-to-noise ratio of the
amplitudes; a debiased sandwich error gives nominal coverage over four decades of $\Tm/\J$. A
zero-mean amplitude prior with a free scale is the same estimator for one component with uniform
noise, and is biased otherwise, by up to four standard deviations in the configuration below.
We apply the estimators to the WMAP nine-year K to V polarization maps with their dense noise
covariances, on four regions defined by geometry with the brightest emission and Tau A masked. On
identical maps the index of a single power law at $|b| > 30^\circ$ outside the North Polar Spur is
\N{prof2.HL} with the profile likelihood, \N{f23two.HL} with a flat amplitude prior stated at 23 GHz
and \N{f30two.HL} with the same prior stated at 30 GHz; the change of convention moves the four
regions by \N{d3023two.range}, which is \N{d3023sd.range} statistical standard deviations. With a
free CMB amplitude the flat-prior marginal has no optimum in the allowed range at high latitude:
it is pulled toward the index at which the synchrotron and CMB spectra are degenerate. A white prior
on the CMB in place of the $\Lambda$CDM covariance quotes a smaller error and, to first order, has a
larger one. The fit passes the tests that use less of the same data (large angular scales only, K and
Ka only, the scatter of the score between 48 patches of sky) within about two of the standard
deviations expected for them. It fails in specific ways: along the CMB spectrum the Galactic plane
has \N{chi.dir.Plane.percent} percent more $\chi^2$ than expected, \pend{H16}; the index from K and
Ka alone is flatter than from four bands at two standard deviations in two regions; and the total
$\chi^2$ is \N{chi.nsig} to \N{flat.chi.nsig} standard deviations below its
expectation. We therefore give the indices as the answer of one model on one set of regions, and not
as a measurement.
\end{abstract}
